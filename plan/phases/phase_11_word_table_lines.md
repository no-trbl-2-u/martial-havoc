# Phase 11 — Word-table lines

> Agent-facing brief. Generated by the loop because the build plan's
> row had none. The 72 Inspirations and 216 Sparks are the sandbox's
> imagination engine and every one of them is a bare word; this phase
> gives each an authored line, and gives the table voice the guide it
> has never had.

## Outcome

Every word in the two d66 word tables carries a line of ours, and the
lines are held to a written guide by a test — the same bargain the
narrator's lines got in 10a.

**Done when:** `world.inspiration-lines` carries 72 records and
`world.spark-lines` carries 216, one per word, each with a `ref` to
the word it reads and a `cite` to the folio the word came from; a
table-voice section exists in `plan/VOICE.md` and `voice.test.ts`
holds all 288 to it; ABOUT's authored count moves by 288; the rules
panel and the beat are untouched.

**Waits on:** Phase 10 in time only. Nothing in Phase 10 is imported
here.
**Cost:** the writing. Three to four weeks by hand; the phase may ship
in tranches (see Decisions).

## What the book gives

- **MH p.59-60, Inspirations.** A d66 table of 72 words in two
  columns, `action` and `subject`: Attack, Investigate, ... The book
  uses them as the Oracle's second half — "if the answer needs
  colour, roll two words and read them together".
- **MH p.60-63, Sparks.** Six d66 tables of 36 words each, numbered 1
  to 6: Mace, Coin, ... The book's own instruction is to roll one d6
  for the table and a d66 for the word.
- **MH p.58, the Oracle.** Where both are called from. The Oracle's
  own 66 answers already carry authored lines
  (`world.oracle-lines`), and those are the model this phase
  follows: a `ref` to the printed row, a line of ours beside it.
- The book never tells the reader what a word *means*. That is the
  whole opening this phase writes into: a word is a prompt, and the
  line is the prompt made usable at the table without closing it.

## Content shape

Two new files, mirroring `world/oracle-lines.json` exactly:

| File | id | kind | records | `ref` target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `data/world/inspiration-lines.json` | `world.inspiration-lines` | `list` | 72 | `inspiration.<table>.<d66>` |
| `data/world/spark-lines.json` | `world.spark-lines` | `list` | 216 | `spark.<n>.<d66>` |

Each record: `{ id, cite, ref, line }`. `id` is `line.` + the ref.
`cite` is the word's own folio (MH p.59-60 for Inspirations, MH
p.60-63 for Sparks) — the line is ours, the word is the book's, and
the cite names where the word came from, as `oracle-lines` does.

Exports in `packages/content/src/world/index.ts`:
`inspirationLines`, `inspirationLineFor(ref)`, `sparkLines`,
`sparkLineFor(ref)` — `byRef` lookups, the same helper
`oracleLineFor` uses. Both added to `counts.ts`'s `authored` list, so
ABOUT's number moves without anything being typed in.

## The table voice — the guide this phase writes

`plan/CRITIQUE.md` carries a standing `[MED]`: 149 authored lines are
governed by no guide at all, because `voice.test.ts` deliberately
scopes itself to the narrator's three homes. Shipping 288 more
unguided lines would take that number to 437 and make it the majority
of the build's content. So this phase writes the second guide rather
than growing the gap.

`plan/VOICE.md` gains a section, **"The table voice"**, and
`voice.test.ts` a second describe block over the two new files:

1. **One sentence.** A word is a prompt; two sentences is a scene.
2. **Twenty words or fewer.** Shorter than the narrator's forty: this
   is a caption, not a telling.
3. **No second person, no Master's name.** Same as his, for the same
   reason: the line must fit whoever is at the table.
4. **No digits, no dice, no rule words** (SKILL, ENDURANCE, LUCK,
   roll, check, Proficiency). The line colours; it does not resolve.
5. **The word itself appears, in its printed spelling.** The line
   reads the word; a line that never says it is a line about
   something else. Case-insensitive, and the printed spelling is the
   one that must appear.
6. **No question marks.** The Oracle asks; the word answers.

Rules 1-4 and 6 are mechanical and go in the test as written. Rule 5
is the one that makes the file honest and is the reason the test can
be trusted: 288 lines cannot be reviewed by eye, and a line that has
drifted off its word is exactly the failure that would survive a
review.

## Decisions made upfront — DO NOT ASK

- **Two files, not one.** The two tables are rolled differently (a
  d66 against a named column; a d6 then a d66) and cited to different
  folios. One file would need a discriminator column to say which,
  which is a join the ids already make.
- **The lines are not Old Ping's.** He narrates what happened; these
  read a word off a table. The existing exclusion in `voice.test.ts`
  is upheld and its comment is amended to name the new guide rather
  than an absence.
- **Tranches are allowed, a partial ship is not.** The phase may be
  written over several commits — Inspirations first (72), then the
  six Spark tables (36 each) — but the row is `[x]` only when all 288
  exist and the test is green over both files. A tranche commit says
  which tables it covers in its body. This is what "a smaller number
  with the fallback named" in the build plan's Done-when means, made
  concrete.
- **No line is reserved.** The build plan's per-phase scope offers the
  operator `[needs-user-call]` on any record; none is taken by
  default, and the loop does not stop to ask. An operator who wants a
  word for themselves marks it after the fact and `/oversight` owns
  the rewrite.
- **The word tables themselves are read-only.** They are
  transcriptions (rule 9); this phase adds lines beside them and
  changes no cell.
- **ABOUT stays one number.** The count is authored lines, and these
  are authored lines. The critique row about one number describing two
  voices is answered by the guide existing, not by splitting the
  count.
