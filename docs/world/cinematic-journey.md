# Appendix A: Philosophy of the open system (p. 81)

The appendix is four sentences long and is quoted in full:[^rulebook]

> This game is based on the philosophy of a game in continuous development. Anyone can contribute to changing the rules, expanding parts of the world, or adding details.
> If you want to contribute with new material, or if you think the price of an item is wrong, or you simply think you have a good idea, join in and edit the text.
> This project came to life thanks to Ruolatori Solitari group: https://www.ruolatorisolitari.it/

The inventory classes this as guidance (R81). It is the licence's spirit stated as design: the book is CC BY-SA 4.0 (p. 93) and a hack of Heroic Havoc (p. 3), and expects to be hacked in turn.[^inventory]

# Appendix B: Martial Havoc - Cinematic Journey (pp. 81-91)

Guidance for pacing, not a rule of play (R82).[^inventory] The book contrasts two modes: "It is possible to experience Solo RPG as a sandbox experience in which the protagonist lives endless adventures, between tables and dice rolls, until the demiurge decides to change the game", and MH-CJ, "an alternative tool for creating stories and bringing them to fruition in 90 minutes of imaginary television time; the three-act theory is used to harness the narrative into a predefined structure, accompanying the player in reliving simple adventures of revenge and magic seen on TV" (p. 81).[^rulebook]

## Vocabulary (p. 82-83)

- The outline is "a simplified outline of the three-act theory, adapted to the cliches of Hong Kong film productions"; narrative tension, "once past the initial phase, tends to rise continuously until it reaches the final climax".
- **Plot point**: "an event in the story that has an impact on the characters or the world, and pushes it in a new direction" (marked with a symbol on the book's diagram).
- **Climax**: "Each act ends with a climax, a point of maximum tension; a point of no return to a new act. The protagonist cannot physically go back, or events have changed him irreversibly."
- **Obstacle**: "the engine that drives the story forward: let's imagine them as a circular movement that starts with the presentation of the obstacle and ends with its resolution." Obstacles may open sub-Obstacles; "It is important not to open too many sub-obstacles and to return to the narrative path to maintain tension towards the objective." "In MH-CJ, consider most sub-obstacles as fights, i.e., the means to continue resolving the main obstacle: entering a building, escaping from a building, convincing someone, etc."[^rulebook]

## Dice versus story (p. 84)

"Although in a film it is possible to decide the number of Obstacles, in Solo RPGs, and more generally in emerging fiction, we do not know how many trials our protagonist will have to overcome, but we can interpret the results of the dice rolls to understand if we have reached a plot point. If the result of the dice roll conflicts with the linear development of the story, ignore the dice. Reach the plot point without lowering the tension."

The book's example: at the start of the third act, with the generals defeated and the boss's door opening, "a roll on the Event table with Rest result, although plausible, would slow down the momentum. Instead, let the Encounter happen and prepare for the finale." The player may draw the outline as they play, "marking the transition to a new act and the achievement of plot points".[^rulebook]

## Act I - Presentation (pp. 84-85)

- Define the world "with its rules (realism, presence of the supernatural, historical time)" and introduce the characters.
- **Call/Incident**: "an event that changes the balance of the world and involves the protagonist. It defines the initial Goal." In solo play "the Incident can be generated from the 'Adventures' table pg. 36" (the d66 table lives in [encounters](../rules/encounters.md) or the campaign that uses it).
- **Motivation/Point of No Return**: "the push that convinces the protagonist to accept the call"; if not obvious it "can emerge by playing the first act".
- **Climax**: "the abandonment of the Ordinary World just presented. The protagonist is ready for action and to enter a New World."
- Advice: decide "whether magic is present. If so, go overboard with the special effects; otherwise, remember that a deity can manifest itself with flashes of fire or as an invisible hand." "Also, set concrete goals, avoid politics and complicated romantic entanglements." An extra tool is recommended: Faser "Adventure Generator" (Ruolatori Solitari, https://ruolatorisolitari.itch.io/faser).[^rulebook]

## Act 2 - Confrontation (pp. 85-87)

- "We move into the Other World. It can be a physical place with different rules or a new social context. This is the most dynamic moment in the story."
- **First part**: Obstacles "help us learn more about the Other World and introduce new characters."
- **Central turning point - twist/revelation/progress**: divides the act; "The antagonist may be revealed. The initial Goal may change."
- **Second part**: "The obstacles become more dangerous, the antagonist more pressing, and the goal draws nearer."
- **Crisis/failure/death**: "The goal recedes. Often the loss of a companion or a near-death experience shakes the protagonist's hope".
- **Climax**: "The final goal becomes achievable again. Often, this involves a realization or 'rebirth' of the protagonist."
- "In MH-CJ, the second act represents almost the entirety of the narrative."
- Two structures that can sustain the act alone:
  - **Climbing the pyramid**: "waves of minions, then raise the level of the battle with fewer but more powerful enemies, until reaching the boss at the top of the pyramid" (compare the Oracle's Enemy Type row Minion - Subordinate - Warrior - Boss in [oracle](oracle.md)).
  - **Trial and error**: "the protagonist is immediately confronted with the main challenge, which is impossible to overcome. A sequence of improvements and trials will begin until he is ready (physically or mentally) to overcome the challenge."[^rulebook]

## Act 3 - Resolution (pp. 87-88)

- **Rising Action**: "the protagonist gathers their last strength to achieve the Goal. Obstacles may arise if not previously resolved, but usually no new challenges."
- **Climax**: "in many cases, the boss fight ... The Goal may or may not be achieved (success or failure)."
- **Resolution**: "the adventure is summed up, the protagonist is transformed by the challenges, and all the questions left open in the first two acts are answered." In solo play this is where the player decides whether to "level up and prepare for a new adventure" (see [experience and advancement](../rules/experience-and-advancement.md)). "In MH-CJ, if you decide to end the adventure, you don't have to come up with a meaningful ending; a joyful jump and a freeze frame with the closing credits will suffice." To continue: pick up an unresolved thread, "or answer the question: which figure in the shadows was pulling the strings of the boss you just defeated? And start over."[^rulebook]

## Worked examples with spoilers (pp. 88-91)

"Personal interpretations of the narrative structure of two films, without any claim to academic rigor."[^rulebook]

| Film | Act I | Act II | Act III |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clan of the White Lotus (1980) | Government versus Shaolin rebels; Crane and Tiger against the White Lotus Clan; Crane and Tiger's girlfriend killed. Climax: Tiger flees into hiding. | Training, failed attacks on the monastery, escapes. Twist: a new method (the weaver's style, pressure points). Crisis: near death against the clan leader. Climax: saved by acupuncture, an intuition for the rematch. | Final battle. Climax: sewing needles strike the boss's fatal point. Resolution: revenge complete. |
| Zu: Warriors from the Magic Mountain (1983) | A deserter enters a cave to escape the battlefield. Climax: the cave leads to a magical and dangerous world. | Saved by a swordsman, seeks to be his pupil; a warrior monk and disciple as rivals. Twist: two magical twin swords are the only way to destroy the Blood Demon's soul. The monk knocked out, the swordsman poisoned and turning demon; the Ice Queen slows the poison. Crisis: both masters out. Climax: the young heroes overcome their differences. | The swordsman-turned-demon hinders the quest; the Ice Queen's sacrifice stops him. Climax: the Blood Demon defeated with the twin swords. Resolution: victory of good, personal growth. |

The book closes the appendix with a hand-drawn tension curve for Zu (p. 91), read by the inventory as: Civil war -> The escape -> entrance to a new world -> To survive -> To be accepted -> Boss fight -> Find a cure -> New goal -> Masters' loss -> The disciples unite -> The quest for the sword -> Mini boss fight -> Boss fight -> Victory.[^inventory]
